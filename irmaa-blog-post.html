In 2025, $185 is the lowest monthly premium for Part B Medicare Coverage. Individuals and couples with high incomes have to pay an income-related monthly adjustment amount (IRMAA).

Approximately 8% of Medicare Part B enrollees have incomes high enough to be impacted by IRMAA.

A single medicare beneficiary with an income of $106,000 would pay $185 x 12 = $2,220 in Medicare Part B Premiums per year. If her income increases by just $1 to $106,001 per year, then the first IRMAA threshold kicks in, and she would pay $259 x 12 = $3,108.

That one extra dollar of income caused her to pay $888 in extra medicare premiums. When filing taxes, we’re used to marginal income tax rates of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%. For the purposes of medicare premiums, that last dollar of income has a marginal tax rate of 88,800%?

This is known as a tax cliff, where you pay a disproportionately high amount of tax for an extra dollar of income.

To visualize the cliff, let’s look at a graph of how the annual IRMAA premiums vary by income.
